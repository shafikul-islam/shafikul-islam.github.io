🎯 One goal, two loops

Same robot, two loops that make it reliable

Both lines of my work follow one cycle: sense, model, decide, and check on the real robot. What differs is when the loop closes.

1🔥 Loop 1 · During the build
Improve the process: sense the weld, predict defects, correct heat and speed in real time. + 2🏗️ Loop 2 · Before the build
Improve the environment: measure what makes a cell hard for robots, then design a better one in a digital twin.

🔥 Loop 1 · Process

A robotic WAAM cell that watches itself

In wire arc additive manufacturing, an industrial robot builds a metal part one weld bead at a time, and heat, bead shape, and porosity drift as layers stack up.

My cell adds the senses a skilled welder relies on: vision on the melt pool, sound from the arc, and electrical and thermal signals. Learned models turn these signals into a process state, predict defect risk, and recommend a bounded correction before a defect forms.

🏗️ Loop 2 · Environment

Designing a world robots can work in

Robots fail less when the factory is designed for them. I measure how much lighting, camera pose, clutter, part presentation, fixture design, and layout matter, model that effect as a robotability score R(e), and search for better cell designs in a digital twin before building them. Real trials then recalibrate the twin (sim to real and back).

🤝 Where the loops meet

One cell, both loops

The same thinking returns to the WAAM cell. Torch access, part orientation, and sensor placement are environment choices too. The long-term goal is to design the robot, the process, and the environment together, so manufacturing cells are reliable by design and self-correcting in operation.
