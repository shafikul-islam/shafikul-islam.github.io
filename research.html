🎯 One goal, two levers

Same robot, two ways to make it succeed

A robot fails when the process drifts or when its surroundings work against it. My first focus makes the process watch and correct itself. My second focus redesigns the robot cell so the robot can succeed in the first place, and tests those designs in a digital twin before building them.

1🔥 Improve the process
Sense the weld, predict defects, correct heat and speed in real time. + 2🏗️ Improve the environment
Measure what makes a cell hard for robots, then design a better one.
🔥 Research focus 1 · Process

A robotic WAAM cell that watches itself

In wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM), an industrial robot carries a welding torch and builds a metal part one bead at a time. Each new layer reheats the layers below, so heat, bead shape, and porosity drift during the build.

My cell adds the senses a skilled welder relies on: a camera on the melt pool, an acoustic sensor on the arc, and electrical and thermal signals. Learned models turn these signals into a process state, predict defect risk, and recommend a bounded correction to heat input or travel speed before a defect forms. One example is entropy-aware model predictive control for restart transients (summary).

  • 👁️ Sense: vision, sound, thermal
  • 🧠 Predict: defect risk
  • ⚙️ Act: heat and speed

🏗️ Research focus 2 · Environment

Designing a world robots can work in

Robots fail less when the factory is designed for them. I measure how much lighting, camera pose, clutter, part presentation, fixture design, and layout matter, model that effect as a robotability score R(e), and search for better cell designs in a digital twin before building them. Real trials then recalibrate the twin (sim to real and back).
